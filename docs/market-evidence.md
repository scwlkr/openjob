## OpenJob documented state (not market validation)

The current Master Mission (accessed 16 Oct 2026) says OpenJob has personal Mail, Tasks, Calendar and scoped agents connected through shared APIs/CLI; provider mail remains simulated for some workflows. It records Gmail/Graph/IMAP read translation and mock OAuth, but live production connections are unconfigured; full IMAP TLS/login/MIME and native/release acceptance are unverified. Treat as documented prototype state, not production readiness or demonstrated customer value.

Source: [scwlkr/openjob docs/MASTER_MISSION.md](https://github.com/scwlkr/openjob/blob/main/docs/MASTER_MISSION.md)

## Gap hypothesis (not validated demand)

In this representative comparison, the basic mail→task→calendar workflow is already well served inside Google/Microsoft, and Nextcloud plus desktop PIMs address parts of provider aggregation. A plausible differentiator to test is a user-owned workspace spanning existing provider accounts and personal plus multiple company contexts, with separate external agent identities, revocable resource- and operation-scoped grants, visible action history, common API/CLI, and a complete human workflow that does not require an AI service or provider migration. This is a capability hypothesis from selected official docs; it is not evidence that no competitor offers it, that a segment wants it, or that it is commercially viable.

## Initial customer wedge hypothesis

Independent consultants/fractional operators and small agencies with personal and client/company identities across Google, Microsoft and/or standards-based mail, who repeatedly convert inbound messages into tasks and calendar actions and want a separate assistant identity without blanket access. Start with users/firms able to authorize their own provider connections; Google verification/restricted-scope requirements and Microsoft app-consent/admin controls make broad enterprise readiness an unproven assumption.

## Questions that would validate/refute the hypothesis

1. Have users walk through their most recent cross-account email→task/calendar workflow, including current workaround, frequency, errors and switching cost; avoid leading questions.
2. Which accounts/providers and company/client contexts are involved? Is a personal identity with multiple memberships desirable or would people require strict tenant separation?
3. Which system remains authoritative for each record type: provider mail/calendar/tasks or OpenJob? Are task/calendar records expected to sync both ways?
4. Which agent operations are acceptable without approval (read, label, draft, send, edit/delete, access administration)? What exact per-resource boundaries, revocation latency, action history and recovery controls are prerequisites?
5. Can the target user legally/technically authorize third-party OAuth/IMAP access to work accounts, or does tenant policy block it?
6. What minimum provider set solves one actual workflow? What capabilities are must-have vs acceptable gaps across Gmail, Microsoft Graph, IMAP/SMTP and CalDAV?
7. Would users choose this over existing Workspace/M365 flows, Nextcloud, Thunderbird or Kontact, and why? Test with a concrete prototype/pilot or setup commitment.
8. Would the human Mail/Calendar/Tasks workspace remain useful without AI? What deployment model (hosted/self-hosted/local) would they trust and pay for?

## Unsupported claims to avoid

No TAM/market-size, adoption, demand, willingness-to-pay, usage, customer-interview, security certification, production readiness, or competitor-absence claim was established by this research.
